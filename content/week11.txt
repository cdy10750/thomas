SUBTITLE Talking with Jiana on video
GOAL Handle a whole video call in Bisaya: connection problems, daily news, family greetings, and a sweet goodbye.

DAY 1 | Can you hear me? | Start the call and check the connection.
CULTURE Calling across time zones | Most of your calls happen across a 12-16 hour time difference. Set fixed call times that suit both of you, and start each call with the right greeting for HER time of day.

DAY 2 | Technical trouble | Weak signal, frozen video, call back.
CULTURE Signal in the province | Internet in rural Bohol can be slow, especially in rain. A frozen screen is normal. Keep it light - joking about the signal together is part of long-distance love.

DAY 3 | Missing you | The sweet part of every call.
CULTURE Saying it in Bisaya | Jiana hears 'I love you' in English from movies and songs. Hearing Gihigugma tika from you, in her own language, is different. Use it.

DAY 5 | News and family | Ask what's new and how everyone is.
CULTURE Asking about everyone | A good call includes asking about each family member: Kumusta si Nanay? si Tatay? si Inday? Remembering small news - Dodong's exam, Lola's health - means a lot.

DAY 6 | Hi to the family, and goodbye | Greet Nanay when she appears, send greetings, and end the call.
CULTURE Ending the call | Filipino goodbyes can take a while. Plan the next call (Ugma?), send greetings to the family, and say goodbye more than once. Ayo-ayo and Amping are both 'take care'.
