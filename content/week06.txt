DAY 1 | Like and don't like | Say yes and no to food - politely.
CULTURE A soft no | Saying a flat 'no' to food can feel harsh. Dili kaayo ko ganahan (I don't really like it) plus a smile is much softer. Taking a small taste first is even better.

DAY 2 | Trying new things | Balut, adobo, and asking what something is called.
CULTURE Balut | Balut is sold by street vendors in the evening, often with salt and vinegar. Relatives may offer it as a playful test. Trying a little - or laughing and declining - are both fine.

DAY 3 | Spicy, sweet, salty | Describe tastes so the cook knows what you like.
CULTURE Not very spicy | Boholano food is usually not very spicy - chili is often on the side as sili. Desserts and snacks, on the other hand, are often very sweet.

DAY 4 | Favorites | Name your favorites and praise the cook.
CULTURE Lechon | Bohol and neighboring Cebu are famous for lechon - a whole roast pig with crispy skin. It appears at fiestas, birthdays and big family events. Saying it's your favorite will make you very popular.

DAY 5 | This or that? | Choose between two things, and compare.
CULTURE Kini, kana | kini = this (near me), kana = that (near you). With these two words and a finger to point, you can order anything.

DAY 6 | Drinks and compliments | Handle the tuba offer and give the best compliment of the trip.
CULTURE Tuba and toasts | At gatherings, men may pass around tuba or beer in one shared glass (tagay). You can join with a little, or say you don't drink - just say it with a smile.
