DAY 1 | Arriving and leaving | The two questions you'll hear on every trip: when did you come, and when do you go?
CULTURE Mouli | Mouli means going home. When you tell the family when you're leaving, expect them to say it's too short and ask when you'll come back. It's their way of saying they like having you.

DAY 2 | How long? | Answer how long you're staying and how long the trip takes.
CULTURE Ka links numbers | In Bisaya you join a number and a noun with ka: duha ka semana (two weeks), tulo ka adlaw (three days), baynte ka oras (twenty hours).

DAY 3 | What time is it? | Ask and tell the time, here and in Bohol.
CULTURE Spanish clock | Time is told with Spanish numbers: alas siyete (7), alas otso (8), alas nuwebe (9), alas diyes (10), alas onse (11), alas dose (12). You'll also hear English: 'seven o'clock'.

DAY 4 | Counting | Count to ten, and count down the days until you see Jiana.
CULTURE Three number systems | Bisaya numbers (usa, duha, tulo) for counting things; Spanish numbers (uno, dos, tres) for time, age and often prices; English numbers everywhere else. Recognizing all three is enough.

DAY 5 | Making plans | Tomorrow, what time, early start.
CULTURE Filipino time | Start times are often flexible: a plan for 8 might start at 9. But for trips to the airport or the ferry, people do leave early. Ask Unsang orasa? and plan to be ready.
