DAY 1 | Going to Mass | Accept the invitation to church and behave like a pro.
CULTURE Church in Bohol | Most Boholanos are Catholic and Sunday Mass is a family event. Dress neatly (no shorts or tank tops), and if you're not Catholic, stay seated during communion - nobody will mind. Bohol's old stone churches, like Baclayon, are among the oldest in the country.

DAY 2 | Fiesta! | The biggest day of the year in every town.
CULTURE The town fiesta | Each town celebrates its patron saint once a year. Houses open their doors, there's lechon, music and a procession. Families invite relatives and friends to eat - you may visit three or four houses in one day.

DAY 3 | Dance invitation | Inday wants you on the dance floor. Here's what you'll say.
CULTURE Everyone dances | At fiestas and parties older people dance too. Trying, even badly, earns more respect than refusing. Hinay-hinay lang works here as well.

DAY 4 | Karaoke | Videoke is unavoidable. Make your one song count.
CULTURE Videoke | Almost every party has a videoke machine that scores your singing. Low scores get laughs, not judgment. Choose a song you know - American classics from the 70s and 80s are very popular.

DAY 5 | Meeting guests | Cousins, aunts, neighbors - meet them all.
CULTURE Ig-agaw | Filipino families count second and third cousins as close family, and family friends become Tito and Tita (uncle and aunt). You'll meet many people. Nobody expects you to remember every name.

DAY 6 | A birthday for Lola | Greet Grandma, ask her age, and thank the hosts.
CULTURE Lolo and Lola | Grandparents are deeply respected. Greet them first, with a mano. Big birthdays (60, 70, 80) are huge family celebrations with food for everyone in the neighborhood.
