TITLE Kaon na!
SUBTITLE At the dinner table
GOAL Understand the food offers that come at every meal, accept and refuse politely, and praise the cooking.

C 01 | Nanay | Dinner table | Kaon na, Thomas! | Come and eat, Thomas! | Kaon = eat. na = now. You will hear this at every single meal.
C 02 | Thomas | Dinner table | Sige, Nay. Moadto na ko. | Okay, Mom. I'm coming. | moadto = go (there). na ko = now I... | check: Natural reply when called to the table?
C 03 | Tatay | Dinner table | Kaon pa! | Eat some more! | pa = more / still. Hosts keep offering. It's how they show love.
C 04 | Thomas | Dinner table | Busog na ko. | I'm full now. | busog = full (after eating).
C 05 | Nanay | Dinner table | Gamay ra imong gikaon! | You only ate a little! | gamay ra = only a little. gikaon = what was eaten. | check: Natural for 'you ate so little'?
C 06 | Thomas | Dinner table | Busog na gyud ko, Nay. Salamat. | I'm really full, Mom. Thank you. | gyud = really. Add it to sound sincere.
C 07 | Nanay | Dinner table | Lami? | Is it good? | lami = delicious. The cook will ask this - watch her face.
C 08 | Thomas | Dinner table | Lami kaayo! | It's very delicious! | kaayo = very. The best compliment you can give the cook.
C 09 | Inday | Dinner table | Unsa'y gusto nimo? | What would you like? | gusto = want / like. nimo = you (your).
C 10 | Thomas | Dinner table | Kini, palihug. | This one, please. | kini = this. Point at the dish as you say it.
C 11 | Kuya | Dinner table | Kaon og daghan, Thomas! | Eat a lot, Thomas! | daghan = a lot. og = some / of.
C 12 | Nanay | Dinner table | Kuha pa og kan-on. | Take more rice. | kan-on = cooked rice. Rice comes with every meal, even breakfast.
C 13 | Thomas | Dinner table | Tama na, salamat. | That's enough, thank you. | tama na = that's enough. Smile and pat your stomach.
C 14 | Jiana | Dinner table | Gigutom ka? | Are you hungry? | gutom = hungry.
C 15 | Thomas | Dinner table | Oo, gigutom na ko. | Yes, I'm hungry now. | Swap Oo for Dili (no) if you're not.
C 16 | Nanay | Dinner table | Inom og tubig. | Drink some water. | inom = drink. tubig = water.
C 17 | Thomas | Dinner table | Pwede ko mangayo og tubig? | Can I have some water? | pwede = can / may (from Spanish puede). mangayo = ask for.
C 18 | Dodong | Dinner table | Kamot o kutsara? | Hands or spoon? | kamot = hand. kutsara = spoon. Eating with your hands is called kinamot.
C 19 | Thomas | Dinner table | Sulayan nako! | I'll try it! | Use this whenever someone offers you something new. They will love it.
C 20 | Tatay | Dinner table | Mag-ampo sa ta. | Let's pray first. | ampo = pray. sa = first. Many families say grace before eating. | check: Natural for leading grace at the table?
C 21 | Nanay | Dinner table | Kaon na ta! | Let's eat! | ta = we (you and me together).
C 22 | Thomas | Dinner table | Human na ko. | I'm finished. | human = finished / done.
C 23 | Jiana | Video call | Nikaon na ka? | Have you eaten? | Filipinos ask this the way Americans ask 'How are you?'. It shows care.
C 24 | Thomas | Video call | Oo, nikaon na ko. | Yes, I've eaten. | nikaon = ate (completed).
C 25 | Thomas | Dinner table | Unsa ni? | What is this? | ni = this (short for kini). Ask about every new dish.

DAY 1 | Called to the table | The first words of every meal: being called to eat, and saying you're hungry.
CULTURE Mealtimes are the center of the day | Filipino families eat together and often. Being called to eat is not a question. Come to the table quickly - keeping people waiting is the rude part, not the eating.

DAY 2 | Eat more! | The battle every guest fights: more food keeps coming. Here is how to say you're full.
CULTURE Kaon pa! is love | Hosts will offer more food again and again, and say you ate too little even when you didn't. Refusing once is expected. Say you're full, smile, and say thank you - two or three times is normal.

DAY 3 | It's delicious! | Praise the food, ask what a dish is, and agree to try something new.
CULTURE Praise the cook | Saying Lami kaayo! to whoever cooked is one of the fastest ways to win over a Filipino family. Trying everything at least once matters more than finishing it.

DAY 4 | Choosing and enough | Point to what you want, take more rice, and politely stop.
CULTURE Rice, rice, rice | A meal without rice is just a snack for many Filipinos. Expect rice at breakfast, lunch and dinner. When you've had enough, Tama na, salamat with a hand over your plate is the polite stop sign.

DAY 5 | Drinks, grace, and 'have you eaten?' | Ask for water, join in grace, and answer the most common Filipino greeting.
CULTURE Nikaon na ka? | 'Have you eaten?' is a greeting, not a dinner invitation. Answer it like 'How are you?': Oo, nikaon na ko. Jiana may ask it on almost every video call.

DAY 6 | Hands, a big appetite, and done | Eat the local way, take Kuya's advice, and say you're finished.
CULTURE Kinamot: eating with your hands | At a beach picnic or with grilled fish, many Boholanos eat with their right hand. Use your fingertips and push food with your thumb. If you try it, everyone will notice - in a good way.
