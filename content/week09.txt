DAY 1 | Shower time | The morning shower - and what to say when there's no hot water.
CULTURE Tabo and timba | Many homes shower with a bucket (timba) and a dipper (tabo) instead of a showerhead, and water is usually cold. It's refreshing in the heat. Ask Jiana to show you how it's done.

DAY 2 | Offering help | Offer to help, get refused, and offer again.
CULTURE The guest dance | As a guest you'll first be told to sit down. Offering again shows good manners. Small jobs - washing dishes, carrying things, sweeping - make you part of the family.

DAY 3 | Taking it easy | Sit down, relax, and ask what the plan is.
CULTURE Nothing planned is a plan | Many days in Bohol are slow: sitting, chatting, eating, napping. It's quality time. You don't need to fill every day with sightseeing.

DAY 4 | Sleeping and mosquitoes | Mornings after the night, mosquitoes, and saying good night.
CULTURE Mosquitoes | Mosquitoes are worst at dusk. Close doors and windows in the evening, use repellent, and sleep under a fan or net. Telling the family Gipaak ko sa lamok gets you help quickly.

DAY 5 | Chores together | Laundry, putting things away, and following simple directions.
CULTURE Laundry day | Laundry is often washed by hand and hung outside. Helping hang clothes or carry the basket is a simple way to spend time with Nanay - even without many words.

DAY 6 | Evenings: TV and brownouts | Watch TV with the family and stay calm when the power goes out.
CULTURE Brownouts | Power outages still happen in parts of Bohol, especially in storms. Families keep candles, flashlights and fans with batteries. Charge your phone when you can.
