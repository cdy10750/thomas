DAY 1 | Who's who | Jiana introduces her family. Learn the family titles and a warm greeting.
CULTURE Family titles instead of names | In the Philippines you rarely call older relatives by their first name. Use the title: Nanay or Nay, Tatay or Tay, Kuya for older brothers. Younger ones are often called Inday (girls) and Dodong or Dong (boys).

DAY 2 | Brothers, sisters, and kids | Answer the family-size questions everyone asks.
CULTURE Big families | Many Boholano families have four, five or more children, and cousins are counted almost like siblings. Don't be surprised if 'my brother' turns out to be a cousin.

DAY 3 | Age and work | Age and job questions come early. Here are the questions and short answers.
CULTURE Age is respect | Age questions help people choose how to address you. At around fifty you may hear younger people call you Sir, Kuya, or even Tito (uncle). It's a sign of respect, not a comment on your age.

DAY 4 | Where do you live? | Talk about where your family lives and where you live.
CULTURE Naa and Wala | Naa means 'there is' or 'yes, I have'; Wala means 'there isn't' or 'no, I don't'. With these two words you can answer most 'do you have...?' questions.

DAY 5 | Boyfriend, and the big question | How Jiana introduces you, and how to survive 'When are you getting married?'
CULTURE Kanus-a mo magminyo? | Relatives ask couples about marriage often and openly. It's friendly curiosity. A smile and a light answer like Pangutan-a si Jiana! keeps it fun. Talk with Jiana beforehand about how she'd like you to answer.

DAY 6 | Warm words | Compliments, a father's request, and being welcomed into the family.
CULTURE Gwapo and Gwapa | Filipinos tease with compliments all the time. Expect Gwapo man ka! from aunties and laugh along. Returning it - Gwapa pud ka! - is the right move.
