TITLE At the Market
SUBTITLE Shopping, the market, and prices
GOAL Ask prices, understand the answer, bargain a little, and pay - or let the family handle it.

C 01 | Thomas | Market | Pila ni? | How much is this? | pila = how much. ni = this. The most useful market phrase.
C 02 | Tindera | Market | Singkwenta pesos. | Fifty pesos. | Prices are usually said in Spanish or English numbers.
C 03 | Thomas | Market | Mahal kaayo! | That's too expensive! | mahal = expensive. Say it with a smile - it's part of the game.
C 04 | Thomas | Market | Pwede hangyo? | Can we bargain? | hangyo = ask for a discount. Polite at markets, not in malls.
C 05 | Tindera | Market | Sige, kwarenta na lang. | Okay, just forty. | na lang = just, only. kwarenta = forty.
C 06 | Thomas | Market | Paliton nako ni. | I'll buy this. | palit = buy. | check: Natural?
C 07 | Thomas | Market | Duha ka buok, palihug. | Two pieces, please. | buok = piece.
C 08 | Tindera | Market | Unsa pa? | What else? | pa = more, else.
C 09 | Thomas | Market | Mao ra. | That's all. | mao ra = that's it.
C 10 | Nanay | Market | Ayaw palit diri, mahal. | Don't buy here, it's expensive. | ayaw = don't. Foreigners often get a higher price.
C 11 | Nanay | Market | Ako na lang ang mohangyo. | Let me do the bargaining. | Let her - she'll get a better price.
C 12 | Thomas | Living room | Asa ta mamalit? | Where are we going shopping? | mamalit = go shopping.
C 13 | Jiana | Living room | Moadto ta sa merkado. | We're going to the market. | merkado = market. Also: mall.
C 14 | Thomas | Market | Barato! | That's cheap! | barato = cheap.
C 15 | Tindera | Market | Presko ni, bag-o lang nakuha. | This is fresh, just caught. | presko = fresh. You'll hear it at every fish stall. | check: Natural for fish?
C 16 | Thomas | Market | Pila ang usa ka kilo? | How much for one kilo? | Fish, fruit and rice are sold by the kilo.
C 17 | Tindera | Market | Usa ka gatos ang kilo. | One hundred a kilo. | gatos = hundred. You may also hear 'one hundred'.
C 18 | Thomas | Market | Naa moy sukli? | Do you have change? | sukli = change (money). Small stalls often don't.
C 19 | Tindera | Market | Wala koy sukli. | I don't have change. | Carry small bills: 20, 50, 100 pesos.
C 20 | Kuya | Market | Ako na'y mobayad. | I'll pay. | bayad = pay.
C 21 | Thomas | Market | Dili, ako na. | No, I've got it. | Expect a friendly fight over the bill.
C 22 | Inday | Market | Palit ta og pasalubong! | Let's buy pasalubong! | pasalubong = gifts you bring back from a trip.
C 23 | Thomas | Market | Para kang Nanay. | It's for Mom. | para = for. kang = to, for (before a name).
C 24 | Thomas | Market | Asa ang CR? | Where's the restroom? | CR = comfort room. Everyone says CR.
C 25 | Nanay | Market | Bitbita ni. | Carry this. | bitbit = carry by hand.

DAY 1 | How much? | Ask the price, understand it, and react.
CULTURE Pesos and prices | Prices are in pesos. Small markets have no price tags, so you ask Pila ni? Numbers come back in Spanish (singkwenta, kwarenta) or English. If you don't catch it, Balika, palihug works here too.

DAY 2 | Bargaining | Ask for a better price, and let Nanay take over.
CULTURE Hangyo | Bargaining is normal at markets and with tricycle drivers, but keep it friendly and small. A foreigner's face can raise the price - Nanay or Jiana will often bargain for you. Let them.

DAY 3 | Buying | Say what you want, how many, and when you're done.
CULTURE Buok | buok means 'piece'. Usa ka buok (one piece), duha ka buok (two pieces). Vendors may also count in English - 'two pieces' - which is fine.

DAY 4 | At the wet market | Fresh fish and prices by the kilo.
CULTURE The wet market | The public market (merkado or tabo) opens early. The freshest fish is gone by mid-morning. It is noisy, wet and wonderful - wear sandals you don't mind getting dirty.

DAY 5 | Paying | Change, small bills, and the fight over who pays.
CULTURE Who pays? | Family members may insist on paying, and you may want to pay. A friendly back-and-forth is normal. Discuss with Jiana beforehand how you'd like to handle money with the family.

DAY 6 | Gifts and practical questions | Pasalubong, who it's for, the restroom, and carrying the bags.
CULTURE Pasalubong | Bringing gifts back from a trip is expected. Bohol favorites include peanut kisses cookies and calamay (a sweet sticky treat in a coconut shell). Gifts from America for the family are also treasured.
