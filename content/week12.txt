DAY 1 | Thank you for everything | Thank-yous that go beyond Salamat.
CULTURE Utang kabubut-on | Filipinos value utang kabubut-on - a debt of gratitude that is remembered for years. A sincere, specific thank-you to Nanay and Tatay before you leave will be talked about long after.

DAY 2 | Happy and sad | Name your feelings and say why.
CULTURE It's okay to show feelings | Goodbyes in Filipino families are emotional - tears at the airport are normal for men and women. Saying Masulub-on ko is honest, and it tells them how much the visit meant.

DAY 3 | Missing each other | Tell them you'll miss them - and that you won't forget.
CULTURE Mingaw | Mingaw is that heavy, lonely feeling of missing people. You'll hear Mingawon mi nimo (We'll miss you) many times on your last day. Answer in kind.

DAY 4 | Come back! | Promise to return. Hear that the door is always open.
CULTURE Balik ka | 'Come back' is the warmest thing a Filipino host can say. Answering Mobalik gyud ko - and meaning it - is the perfect reply.

DAY 5 | Staying in touch | Texting, chatting, the airport ride, and something sweet for Jiana.
CULTURE Messenger family groups | Many families have a group chat. Ask to be added. Even a short Maayong buntag! in the group keeps you close to everyone between visits.

DAY 6 | Goodbye at the door | The final words as you leave the house.
CULTURE Leaving the house | Before leaving, give each elder a mano one last time. Family members may walk you to the car and wave until you're out of sight. Wave back until you can't see them.
