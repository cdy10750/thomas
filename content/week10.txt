DAY 1 | Heading out | Find out where you're going and how.
CULTURE Bohol's sights | Favorites for visitors: the Chocolate Hills, the tiny tarsiers (one of the smallest primates in the world) in Corella, the Loboc River cruise, and white-sand beaches on Panglao. Families love to show them off.

DAY 2 | On the motorbike | Rides in Bohol are often on a motorbike or tricycle.
CULTURE Habal-habal | Motorbikes carry whole families in Bohol, and motorbike taxis (habal-habal) go where cars can't. Wear a helmet when you can, and don't be shy about asking the driver to slow down.

DAY 3 | Far, near, stop | Ask how far, and ask to stop.
CULTURE Kilometers and minutes | Distances are given in kilometers or in time, and 'near' can be generous. If you need a break - a restroom, a drink, a photo - just ask to stop. Families are happy to.

DAY 4 | Directions and gas | Left, right, straight - and the gas station.
CULTURE Wala, two meanings | wala means 'left' in directions and 'none / not' everywhere else. Context makes it clear: Liko sa wala = turn left; Wala koy sukli = I have no change.

DAY 5 | Photos and the beach | Pictures, the sea, and swimming.
CULTURE Picture, picture | Filipinos love photos - group photos, selfies, photos with you. Say yes to them; they'll be shared with the whole family by evening.

DAY 6 | Leaving and coming back | Promise Nanay you'll be careful, and tell her when you'll be home.
CULTURE Pag-amping | Families worry about travelers. Telling Nanay where you're going and when you'll be back - and texting if you're late - is a sign of respect.
